## User Profile

### Primary ICP

**Wellness-aligned knowledge workers on Apple Silicon Macs** who already believe breathing affects performance/calm, and who spend long stretches in focused screen work.

**They are already doing at least one of:**

- breathwork (coherent breathing, box breathing, pranayama, Buteyko, etc.)
- meditation / mindfulness
- HRV / Oura / Whoop / Garmin-style self-tracking
- nervous-system regulation practices (somatics, vagal tone content, etc.)

**Key trait:** they don’t need convincing that breathing matters — they need help _remembering to do it while working._

### “Job to be Done”

> “While I’m deep in screen focus, help me _return to a healthy breathing rhythm_ without breaking flow.”

### The pain (what they feel)

- “I look up and realize I’ve been holding my breath.”
- chest tightness, subtle anxiety, jaw/neck tension during computer work
- afternoon crash or agitation that they associate with stress + shallow breathing
- they _know_ techniques but forget to apply them when absorbed

### Why existing solutions fail

- Timer reminders are too interruptive and get turned off
- guided breathwork sessions are “point interventions,” not continuous support
- phone-based cues don’t help because the apnea happens at the computer
- wearables measure, but don’t continuously guide behavior in-the-moment

### What makes Downshift compelling (value prop)

- ambient, always-on cue **on the same screen where the problem happens**
- **continuous** intervention that doesn’t demand attention
- breath cadence feels like a “background metronome” for the nervous system

---

## Persona 1: The Coherent Breather (best first users)

**Profile:** already practices coherent breathing (5–6 breaths/min), maybe tracks HRV, wants calm focus.
**Use case:** coding, writing, research, design work blocks.
**Trigger moment:** “I keep forgetting to breathe when I’m locked in.”
**What they want:** subtle, reliable pacing; minimal settings; no gamification.
**Success looks like:** “I notice my breath more during work; I feel calmer by default.”

**Best messages**

- “Coherent breathing, but ambient—while you work.”
- “A tiny on-screen rhythm that brings you back to breath without interruptions.”

---

## Persona 2: The Somatics / Nervous-System Regulator

**Profile:** therapy-adjacent, somatics, breath as regulation; dislikes rigid protocols.
**Use case:** email + meetings + high-stress tasks.
**Trigger:** “I get tense at my desk and forget my body.”
**What they want:** gentle “downshift” cue; low cognitive load; easy pause.
**Objection risk:** visual cue might feel overstimulating if too noticeable.
**Success:** “Less tension; more embodied during work.”

**Best messages**

- “A gentle cue to downshift your nervous system while you’re on-screen.”

---

## Persona 3: The Biohacker / Tracker

**Profile:** Oura/Whoop, experiments with interventions; likes measurable habits.
**Use case:** structured work blocks; wants retention/consistency.
**Trigger:** “My HRV tanks when I’m stressed at the computer.”
**What they want:** simple tool now; later wants metrics + integrations.
**Success:** “I actually _use_ breathing consistently during work.”

**Best messages**

- “A continuous breathing intervention for desk work (not another 5-minute session).”

---

## Exclusions (for now)

Avoid early:

- people who are skeptical about breathwork (high education cost)
- people who need accessibility accommodations for motion/light sensitivity (until you have more controls)
- Windows-first users (until you ship another platform)

---

## Purchase/Install motivation (why they’ll try an alpha)

- curiosity + “this is exactly my problem”
- desire for a _continuous_ cue, not a reminder
- they enjoy experimenting with small tools
- they prefer lightweight, single-purpose Mac utilities

---

## Objections you should expect (and how to answer)

1. **“Will this distract me?”**
   _Answer:_ “Designed to be ignorable; it fades into the background. Try it for one work block.”
2. **“Not notarized feels sketchy.”**
   _Answer:_ “Alpha disclosure + open source + clear install steps + checksums.”
3. **“Why not just use a timer?”**
   _Answer:_ “Timers interrupt. Downshift stays present and nonverbal.”
4. **“I already do breathwork.”**
   _Answer:_ “This is for the hours between sessions—when you forget.”
